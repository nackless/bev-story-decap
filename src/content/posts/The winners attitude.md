## The winners’ attitude

Winning is largely a game of attitude. Talent, hard work, preparation, opportunity and circumstance all matter, but the first step towards getting anywhere is believing that you can get there.

That belief gives you direction. It allows you to act, persist and recover when things become difficult. In that sense, believing may be the most important first step towards achieving.

However, belief does not simply appear from nowhere. It is closely linked to confidence, and confidence is often built through good preparation, repeated experience and the right circumstances. When you know you have done the work, understand the subject and are ready for the likely challenges, it becomes easier to trust yourself.

## Attitude and circumstance

My family history has shown me both the power and the limits of attitude.

I was born into a middle-class family in a country that had endured two centuries of colonial rule and then the trauma of Partition. For my parents’ generation, security and even survival could feel like luxuries. My grandparents lost their siblings during the violence that followed Partition. My grandmother found my grandfather among the dead and somehow brought him to the newly formed India.

Against that background, my father’s ambition to become a doctor seemed almost impossible to others. People laughed at him. He did not have a clear roadmap or access to the guidance he needed, but he had the will to pursue it. His attitude helped take him where he wanted to go.

Yet determination alone does not remove every barrier. His journey was full of hurdles that better circumstances—access to information, mentorship, financial security, or supportive institutions—could have reduced. He had the will and the work ethic, but often received too little support, too late.

This is why success cannot be explained simply by saying that someone “wanted it more”. Some people begin with enormous advantages: confidence, financial safety, connections, permission to fail and the expectation that they belong in influential spaces. A person such as Donald Trump, for example, entered life with exceptional resources and reinforcement of his own confidence. That does not make effort irrelevant, but it does change what is possible and how readily someone can take risks.

## How this affects me

My father’s experiences also shaped how I see authority. He taught me to be careful and not to question people in power, because people with authority had tried to damage his career and nearly succeeded.

That lesson may once have been protective, but it has also affected my confidence. I can become quiet in front of people I see as more senior, more knowledgeable or more powerful than me. When I feel judged or challenged, I may lose the ability to think as clearly and directly as I normally can.

This is not necessarily a lack of ability. It is a learned response to perceived threat: a fear of being exposed, dismissed or harmed by authority. Recognising it is important, because it means the problem can be addressed.

## Confidence as a skill

Confidence is not the belief that you will know everything or never make mistakes. It is the trust that you can face uncertainty, think through a question and respond honestly and competently.

Preparation remains essential. Prepare thoroughly, anticipate questions, know your evidence and make sure you are in the best possible position. But there will always be more to know, and no amount of preparation can eliminate every difficult question.

The goal is therefore not perfect knowledge. It is the ability to remain steady when challenged.

A useful shift is to replace “I must have the answer immediately” with: “I can think this through; I can ask for clarification; I can say what I know; and I can find out what I do not know.” That is not weakness. It is professional confidence.

## The central principle

To achieve, you need many things: ability, hard work, preparation, support, opportunity and favourable circumstances. But you also need a winners’ attitude.

That attitude means refusing to let fear make your decisions. It means confronting authority without assuming that authority is always right. It means preparing well, trusting your own judgement and recognising that you have a right to take up space in the room.

Winning is not always about being the loudest person or having the easiest path. Often, it is about carrying forward the determination of those who came before you, while giving yourself the confidence and permission that they may not have had.
